Legal Weed? Depends on Where You Live

Where legal cannabis is available depends on your county.

By Baron Carr

December 2025

Walk down almost any commercial strip, and you’ll likely see a dispensary sign or catch the smell of marijuana. But many of those storefronts are not licensed. Depending on where you live, finding licensed cannabis shops can still be a challenge.

New York’s adult-use cannabis market is no longer new.

The map shows wide gaps in access. Some counties have multiple legal options nearby. Others have none. Erie County ranks among the highest in per-capita access, while places like Putnam County have no licensed adult-use dispensaries established.

“If you Google a dispensary or Weedmaps, that’s the standard for locating dispensaries,” said Ted Crawford, owner of By Any Other Name Dispensary in Clinton Hill, Brooklyn.

He also noted that illegal dispensaries are common in neighborhoods, and there are shops that get shut down and then re-opened through a loophole or under a different name. This causes a problem for legally licensed establishments.

“We can’t write off payroll or rent. That’s a killer.”

The Trump administration is weighing in on changes. Federal changes could reclassify marijuana to a less restrictive federal schedule and ease research and medical access.

For now, where you live still determines access to legal weed.

Sources

• New York State Office of Cannabis Management, Current OCM Licenses dataset
• U.S. Census Bureau, ACS Table S0101 (Age and Sex, Adults 18+)
• Interview: Ted Crawford, By Any Other Name Dispensary (Clinton Hill, Brooklyn)

Methodology

Rates were calculated by dividing the number of active adult-use retail dispensaries in each county by the adult population (age 18 and older), then multiplying by 100,000. Counties with no dispensaries are shown as zero, not missing data.
